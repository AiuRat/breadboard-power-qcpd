# Circuit Operation Description of the Combined Power Module

This document contains a detailed technical description of the operation of the electrical schematic for the "Combined power module for solderless breadboards supporting QC/PD".

<p align="center">
  <img src="/docs/images/breadboard-power-qcpd-schematic.png" width="850" alt="Device Schematic Diagram">
</p>

## 1. Purpose and General Architecture

The device is designed to provide stable multi-channel power to solderless breadboards during prototyping and debugging of electronic equipment. The module converts the input voltage from an external charger or an external battery (Power Bank) supporting fast charging protocols into a set of stabilized voltages:
* **Fast charging channel (VBUS):** adjustable voltage of $5\text{ V}$, $9\text{ V}$, $12\text{ V}$, $15\text{ V}$, or $20\text{ V}$ (determined by the trigger request).
* **Low-voltage logic power channel:** fixed voltage of $+3.3\text{ V}$.
* **Negative voltage channel (dual-polarity power supply):** fixed voltage of $-15\text{ V}$.

---

## 2. Principle of Operation of Functional Circuit Blocks

The electrical schematic of the device consists of five main interconnected blocks:

### 2.1. USB Type-C Input Block
Module operation begins with applying external power to the **`X1`** connector (type *TYPEC-304-ACP16*). 
* Contacts `A4/B9` and `B4/A9` are combined into a common input power rail **`VBUS`**.
* Contacts `A1/B12` and `B1/A12` are connected to the system ground **`GND`**.
* Channel configuration contacts `CC1` and `CC2` are routed to the fast charging trigger for negotiations with the power source.
* The LED **`VD2`** (with current-limiting resistor `R10` of $1\text{ k}\Omega$) serves for visual indication of the input voltage presence on the `VBUS` rail.

### 2.2. Fast Charging Protocol Negotiator Block (QC/PD)
The key element communicating with the adapter is the trigger IC **`DD1`** of type **`CH224K`**. It supports hardware protocols for USB Power Delivery (PD3.0/2.0), Quick Charge (QC3.0/2.0), and others.
* Signal lines `DP` (D+) and `DM` (D-), as well as configuration lines `CC1` and `CC2`, are connected directly to the corresponding pins of `DD1`.
* The trigger's operating logic is determined by the state of its configuration inputs `CFG1`, `CFG2`, and `CFG3`. These inputs are pulled up to the internal power rail `VDD` by resistors `R5`, `R6`, `R7` of $10\text{ k}\Omega$ and are controlled by a three-position DIP switch **`SA2`** (*DS1040-03RN*).
* By closing the corresponding contacts of the `SA2` switch to ground (`GND`), the user manually sets the requested voltage level (from $5\text{ V}$ to $20\text{ V}$ dev).

### 2.3. $+3.3\text{ V}$ Linear Regulator Block
For reliable power supply of microcontrollers and digital sensors, a high-power low-dropout (LDO) linear regulator **`DA2`** of type **`LD1086DT33TR`** (TO-252 package) is used.
* Voltage from the `VBUS` rail is supplied to the `IN` input of the regulator. Although `VBUS` can rise up to $20\text{ V}$, the `LD1086` IC safely tolerates input voltages up to $30\text{ V}$ .
* To suppress high-frequency noise and transient processes, ceramic capacitors **`C4`** ($10\text{ }\mu\text{F}$) and **`C5`** ($10\text{ }\mu\text{F}$) are installed at the input and output of the regulator.

### 2.4. $-15\text{ V}$ Inverting DC-DC Converter Block
Negative voltage is generated by a switching buck-boost converter based on the classic PWM controller **`DA1`** of type **`MC34063AG-S08-R`** in an inverting configuration.
* **Principle of inversion:** When the internal power switch transistor of the IC is on, current flows through the inductor **`L1`** ($220\text{ }\mu\text{H}$), storing energy in its magnetic field. The Schottky diode **`VD1`** (*15MQ040N*) is closed at this moment. When the transistor turns off, the inductance tends to maintain the current direction, the self-induction EMF changes polarity, the diode `VD1` opens, and negative charge is transferred to the output capacitor **`C3`** ($47\text{ }\mu\text{F}$).
* **Overcurrent protection:** Resistor **`R3`** ($0.22\text{ }\Omega$) installed between the `DRC/IPK` pins and the power input sets the peak inductor current limit threshold, preventing converter failure during a short circuit.
* **Feedback:** The output voltage is monitored by a comparator through a divider on resistors **`R4`** ($910\text{ }\Omega$), **`R5`** ($750\text{ }\Omega$), and **`R1`** ($10\text{ k}\Omega$), connected to the feedback input `CIIN` (Pin 5). Capacitor **`C1`** ($2.2\text{ nF}$) sets the switching frequency of the PWM generator ($\approx 97.5\text{ kHz}$).

### 2.5. Output Commutation Scheme and Output Connectors
Distribution of voltages to the left and right rails of the breadboard is carried out using two independent slide switches **`SA1` and `SA3`** (*MS-22D16G2-B*).
* The switches route the physical outputs of the `3.3Vout`, `VBUS`, and `-15out` rails to the output header connectors **`X2` and `X3`** (type *DS1021-2x5*).
* The connectors have a $2.54\text{ mm}$ pitch and are located at the edges of the board in such a way that the module plugs directly into the standard side power rails of a solderless breadboard.

## 3. Switch Configuration Tables

### 3.1. QC/PD Input Voltage Selection (Switch `SA2`)

| `CFG1` | `CFG2` | `CFG3` | Output Voltage `VBUS` |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| $1$ | $-$ | $-$ | **$5\text{ V}$** |
| $0$ | $0$ | $0$ | **$9\text{ V}$** |
| $0$ | $0$ | $1$ | **$12\text{ V}$** |
| $0$ | $1$ | $1$ | **$15\text{ V}$** |
| $0$ | $1$ | $0$ | **$20\text{ V}$** |

*Note: "1" indicates the open state of the switch (high level due to pull-up to VDD), "0" indicates closed to GND.*

### 3.2. Breadboard Power Rail Commutation (Switches `SA1`, `SA3`)

| `SA1` Position | `SA3` Position | Voltage on Left Rail `X2` | Voltage on Right Rail `X3` |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 1 | 1 | $+3.3\text{ V}$ | $-15\text{ V}$ |
| 0 | 1 | $+3.3\text{ V}$ | $+3.3\text{ V}$ |
| 0 | 0 | `VBUS` (from trigger) | `VBUS` (from trigger) |
| 1 | 0 | `VBUS` (from trigger) | $-15\text{ V}$ |
